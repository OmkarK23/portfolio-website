01 / About

My Journey

My Journey

A small-town start, a few borders crossed, and a growing pull toward data, leadership, and building things that matter. Here's how I got here, chapter by chapter.

Growing up in a small town near Pune, tech wasn't handed to me, I had to go find it. That search led me to Savitribai Phule Pune University, where I studied Electronics & Telecommunications Engineering and wrote my first line of Python code. I still remember my first real project, it wasn't polished, but it was mine, and it was the moment code stopped being coursework and started being something I wanted to get good at.

In my third year, I got my first real taste of data science as an intern at TCR. I worked on an employee attrition project, digging into why people were leaving specific organizations, and what the data actually said about it versus what everyone assumed. It was the first time I used data to answer a genuinely open question instead of a textbook one, and it planted the seed for everything that came after.

That same year, my peers elected me Vice President of the E&TC Engineering Student Association. By my final year, I was President and organizing technical workshops, cultural events, and professional development programs for the department. It taught me something no coursework could, how to rally people around a shared goal, and how to actually deliver on one.

After graduating, I joined Weatherford as a Product Analyst, working as the technical liaison between clients and developers. I'd meet virtually with clients to understand issues they were facing, recreate those issues myself when I could, and file the right Jira tickets for the dev team. I also used dashboards and data visualization to show what was happening and what could be improved. I'm proudest of the time I noticed a recurring issue at one client, dug into the data, and found the same pattern showing up across other clients too. Presenting that finding and watching the developers build a fix off the back of it was the moment I felt like I'd actually moved the needle. I loved working as that technical liaison.

Choosing to pursue my Master's at UMBC was bigger than a new degree, it was the first time I'd ever left the country. I chose Information Systems specifically because it let me go deeper into data science and data architecture while also building real project and product management skills, both things I cared about. The coursework covered a wide range, including data science and engineering, product management, cybersecurity, data analytics, SDLC/PDLC, and research-focused case studies. I got a lot of praise along the way for something I didn't expect to be a strength, taking complex technical work and explaining it in a way that was easy for anyone to follow. Outside the classroom, I worked part-time as a Math Tutor through UMBC's Reach Together program, working with middle schoolers from public schools in the county, one of the most rewarding parts of my time here.

I carried my leadership experience into GEARS as Events Lead. One highlight was helping organize a full research symposium, including a poster presentation competition and a 3-Minute Thesis (3MT) competition, where researchers present their work in just three minutes. I helped run the whole symposium and reached out to judges and participants to bring it all together.

Most recently, I worked as a Project Management Intern at eSurgi on Biostabilizer, a personalized, AI-enabled solution for chronic low back pain designed to improve outcomes and reduce the burden on physical therapists. My work centered on customer discovery, and we reached out to over 200 physical therapists to validate whether the problem we were solving actually matched what clinicians experience day to day. Alongside that, I helped shape the value proposition, competitor analysis, go-to-market strategy, and financial projections into a coherent, investor-ready pitch deck, while coordinating a four-person intern team to keep everything on track. The biggest lesson was that a good pitch isn't a collection of slides, it's a structured story where every claim needs evidence behind it, especially in healthcare.

I am actively seeking opportunities in Data Engineering, Data & Business Analysis, and Project Management where I can contribute to data-driven decision-making, process improvement, and high-quality delivery.
